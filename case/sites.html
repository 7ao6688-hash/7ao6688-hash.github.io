Site screening · Hampshire, UK

Finding quiet land with good motorway access

A client wants places close to a motorway for easy access, but away from the loudest traffic noise, inside Hampshire's two national parks. I built a small tool that answers this from open government data, and that anyone can rerun by editing a short settings file.

The question

Developers, planners and relocation advisers often ask the same kind of question: which land is close enough to key infrastructure but outside a constraint zone? Here the constraint is road noise of 75 decibels or more, the loudest band in the government's noise model.

What I did

  1. Selected the motorways from Ordnance Survey's open road network.
  2. Drew a bufferA zone of fixed width drawn around a feature, for example all land within 5 km of a motorway. of 5 km and 10 km around them, each merged into one zone.
  3. Removed the noisiest land: every part of each park inside DEFRA's ≥ 75 dB road-noise zone.
  4. Overlapped the remaining quiet land with each buffer and measured the area.
  5. Checked the answer against my original ArcGIS version of the same analysis.

What the results show

Within 5 km of a motorway and below 75 dB, there are 92.7 km² in the New Forest (16% of the park) and 120.7 km² in the South Downs (7%). Within 10 km, that rises to 277 km² and 351 km². The open-source version agrees with ArcGIS to within 0.01%. The tiny gap comes from how each program draws the curved ends of buffers.

Everything a client might want to change sits in one short settings file: the parks, distances, road types and noise bands. No programming is needed, and the tool explains mistakes in plain language. For example, if a road type is misspelt, it lists the ones it found.

How sure can we be?

  • Noise is modelled, not measured, at 4 m above ground. Land with no noise data counts as quiet, which is how the government data are meant to be read.
  • Only two criteria are used. Real site selection would add land ownership, protected habitats, flood risk and planning rules.

What a client gets

  • Suitable-land layers (GeoPackage) and an area table
  • An overview map
  • A reusable tool and settings file for new areas and criteria
  • Migration of existing ArcGIS/arcpy workflows to open-source Python, with checked results
